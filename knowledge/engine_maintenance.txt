# Engine Maintenance Knowledge

## Engine RPM

Engine RPM represents the rotational speed of the engine crankshaft.

Typical operating ranges:
- Warm idle: 600-900 RPM
- Normal driving: 1,500-3,000 RPM
- High RPM operation should be evaluated against the engine manufacturer's limits.

Abnormal RPM behavior can indicate:
- Vacuum leaks
- Throttle body problems
- Idle control problems
- Crankshaft or camshaft position sensor faults
- Engine misfire

A sudden zero or erratic RPM reading can indicate a sensor or signal problem.

## Engine Temperature

Engine temperature represents the thermal condition of the engine.

Typical operating temperature is approximately 90-105°C, depending on the vehicle and operating conditions.

High engine temperature can be associated with:
- Cooling system problems
- Thermostat failure
- Radiator restriction
- Cooling fan failure
- Water pump problems
- Low coolant level

Persistent overheating requires investigation because excessive temperature can damage engine components.

## Coolant Temperature

Coolant temperature should be evaluated together with engine temperature.

Abnormally high coolant temperature can indicate:
- Thermostat problems
- Cooling fan problems
- Radiator restriction
- Water pump problems
- Insufficient coolant circulation

A coolant temperature trend should be compared with historical vehicle behavior.

## Engine Maintenance Inspection

When abnormal engine telemetry is detected, inspect:
- Cooling system
- Engine operating temperature
- RPM behavior
- Sensor signals
- Engine mounts
- Visible leaks
- Relevant maintenance history

Telemetry should be evaluated together with historical operating behavior rather than using a single measurement alone.
